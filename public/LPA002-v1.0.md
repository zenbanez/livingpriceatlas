# LPA002 — The Living Price Atlas
## Domain Architecture and Foundational Inheritance

**Specification:** LPA002  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** November 25, 2026  
**Classification:** Domain Architecture Specification  
**Status:** Proposed for adoption  
**Predecessor:** LPA001 — Foundational Principles and Epistemic Framework  
**Foundational lineage:** Wisdom Infrastructure, ZenOS, Ugnayan

---

## Preamble

The Living Price Atlas emerges from a foundation that precedes it.

The challenges of preserving knowledge, establishing provenance, bounding authority, reconciling competing interpretations, and sustaining human stewardship are not unique to economic intelligence.

They are foundational challenges of trustworthy intelligence itself.

The Living Price Atlas shall therefore not independently reconstruct the epistemic and operational foundations already established through Wisdom Infrastructure.

Instead, it shall inherit, specialize, and extend those foundations within the domain of economic knowledge and the cost of development.

Its distinct responsibility is to understand the nature of price, the conditions under which economic observations become meaningful, and the relationships through which society develops a more accurate understanding of development costs.

The Atlas is not a reinvention of Wisdom Infrastructure.

It is a realization of Wisdom Infrastructure within a particular domain of human knowledge.

---

## 1. Architectural Declaration

The Living Price Atlas shall be constituted as a domain-specific realization of Wisdom Infrastructure.

It shall inherit general-purpose capabilities for trustworthy intelligence while introducing the domain knowledge, contextual models, relationships, and reasoning processes necessary for economic cost intelligence.

The architecture shall distinguish between:

1. Foundational capabilities that are inherited.
2. Domain semantics that are introduced.
3. Operational capabilities that are specialized.
4. Institutional relationships that are instantiated.
5. Public intelligence that is produced.

This distinction shall prevent unnecessary duplication of foundational mechanisms while preserving the autonomy and integrity of the economic domain.

**The Atlas shall specialize the foundation, not fork its principles.**

## 2. Foundational Lineage

### 2.1 Wisdom Infrastructure

Wisdom Infrastructure establishes the broader purpose of preserving, accumulating, interpreting, and responsibly transmitting human knowledge across time.

Within this lineage, the Living Price Atlas represents the stewardship of economic knowledge.

Its responsibility extends beyond maintaining contemporary prices. It shall preserve the experiences, explanations, contextual conditions, and accumulated lessons through which the economics of development become increasingly understandable.

A price that expires may cease to be operationally useful while remaining historically valuable.

An estimate that proves inaccurate may become a valuable source of understanding when its assumptions and outcomes are preserved.

Wisdom Infrastructure provides the conceptual foundation through which these observations become enduring knowledge rather than disposable data.

### 2.2 ZenOS

ZenOS shall serve as the intended operating substrate of the Living Price Atlas.

Where available and conformant with LPA001, its foundational capabilities shall be inherited rather than independently reproduced.

These include capabilities for:

- Evidence preservation
- Provenance and lineage
- Warrants
- Deliberation
- Bounded authority
- Knowledge continuity
- Accountable human and AI participation

The Living Price Atlas shall introduce domain-specific meanings and requirements upon these capabilities.

It shall not establish a separate epistemic authority merely because its subject matter differs from other implementations of Wisdom Infrastructure.

The inheritance described herein constitutes an architectural requirement. The availability and maturity of individual substrate capabilities shall be established through implementation verification.

### 2.3 Ugnayan

Ugnayan shall provide the intended relational architecture through which the participants of the Atlas interact.

Economic knowledge exists across relationships between suppliers, contractors, engineers, institutions, communities, researchers, and other participants.

These relationships are not incidental to the production of knowledge.

They determine how evidence is generated, how expertise is exercised, how claims are challenged, and how responsibility is assigned.

The Atlas shall instantiate domain-specific participation profiles, relationships, and responsibilities through the applicable Ugnayan architecture.

### 2.4 The Living Price Atlas

The Living Price Atlas shall establish the domain semantics and operational intelligence necessary to transform these inherited capabilities into useful economic understanding.

It shall be responsible for:

- Defining what a price represents.
- Establishing the comparability of economic observations.
- Representing contextual cost conditions.
- Interpreting market forces.
- Producing defensible cost estimates.
- Learning from realized development outcomes.
- Communicating economic knowledge responsibly.

These responsibilities constitute the distinctive domain of the Atlas.

## 4. Foundational Inheritance

### 4.1 Evidence Preservation

The Atlas shall inherit the capability to preserve evidence independently of its subsequent interpretation.

Original documents, observations, recordings, and other evidential artifacts shall remain distinguishable from their extracted representations.

The Atlas shall specialize this capability to accommodate economic and infrastructure evidence, including:

- Historical price references
- Supplier quotations
- Commercial invoices
- Bills of quantities
- Procurement records
- Engineering estimates
- Equipment rental schedules
- Labor cost observations
- Site condition reports
- Project completion records

The evidential artifact shall not be reduced exclusively to the information initially extracted from it.

Future interpretations may reveal relevant knowledge that was not recognized at the time of collection.

### 4.2 Provenance

The Atlas shall inherit foundational provenance mechanisms.

Domain specialization shall establish the economic significance of provenance, including the distinction between:

- The originator of a price observation
- The economic participant represented
- The location and time of observation
- The contributor submitting the evidence
- The process interpreting the evidence
- The steward validating its applicability

These may represent different actors.

A document uploaded by an engineer, issued by a supplier, extracted by AI, and validated by an independent steward shall not be attributed indiscriminately to any one of them.

### 4.3 Warrants

The Atlas shall inherit the foundational warrant architecture of ZenOS.

It shall not redefine the general semantics of a warrant.

Within the economic domain, warrants shall be applied to establish the evidential justification, contextual applicability, and appropriate use of economic claims, consistent with the governing foundational specification.

A claim concerning the reasonable cost of an infrastructure project shall not acquire unrestricted legitimacy merely because it has been generated from a recognized dataset.

Its supporting evidence, assumptions, applicability, and limitations must remain ascertainable.

A warrant supporting the use of a historical benchmark shall not automatically warrant its representation as a contemporary market observation.

Likewise, a warrant supporting a local supplier quotation shall not automatically warrant its extrapolation across an entire region.

The domain specialization shall preserve the distinction between the existence of a claim and the justification for relying upon it.

### 4.4 Deliberation

The Atlas shall inherit foundational deliberation mechanisms for the examination of competing claims, interpretations, and proposed conclusions.

Economic deliberation may be initiated when:

- Comparable suppliers report materially different prices.
- Official benchmarks diverge from contemporary market observations.
- A proposed contextual adjustment lacks sufficient support.
- A historical dataset contains conflicting or ambiguous entries.
- Realized project costs contradict earlier estimates.
- A published conclusion is substantively challenged.

Deliberation shall not be initiated merely to force agreement.

Its purpose shall be to improve the understanding of available evidence, identify relevant distinctions, expose unsupported assumptions, and determine what conclusions are reasonably warranted.

Where uncertainty remains, deliberation may legitimately conclude without establishing a single preferred price.

### 4.5 Bounded Authority

The Atlas shall inherit foundational mechanisms for defining and constraining authority.

Domain participants shall exercise authority only within their legitimate scope of competence and responsibility.

A supplier may possess direct knowledge of its offered prices without possessing authority over regional cost estimates.

An engineer may assess technical comparability without possessing exclusive authority over market conditions.

A government institution may publish an official benchmark without rendering competing observations invalid.

An AI system may identify inconsistencies without possessing authority to declare misconduct.

Authority shall be contextual, bounded, reviewable, and attributable.

### 4.6 Knowledge Continuity

The Atlas shall inherit mechanisms for preserving the historical lineage of knowledge.

Economic observations shall be capable of expiring in applicability without disappearing from the historical record.

Revisions shall preserve the relationships between previous and subsequent interpretations.

The Atlas shall distinguish between:

- What was observed.
- What was known at a particular time.
- What was inferred from that knowledge.
- What was subsequently discovered.
- What was corrected.
- What remains unresolved.

The purpose of continuity is not merely to maintain an archive.

It is to preserve society's ability to learn from the evolution of its understanding.

## 5. Domain Semantics

The distinctive contribution of the Living Price Atlas begins with the establishment of economic domain semantics.

The following conceptual primitives shall provide the initial vocabulary of the Atlas.

They are semantic definitions rather than prescriptions of database tables, software classes, or storage formats.

### 5.1 Economic Object

An Economic Object is the identifiable subject of an economic observation or claim.

It may represent:

- A construction material
- A labor service
- A piece of equipment
- A transportation service
- A construction activity
- A completed infrastructure component
- An entire development project

An Economic Object shall possess sufficient definition to support meaningful identification and comparison.

For infrastructure works, this may require dimensions, material specifications, engineering standards, construction methods, quality requirements, and included or excluded activities.

A generic description shall not be presumed equivalent to a technically specified object.

### 5.2 Price Observation

A Price Observation is an evidentially supported record of a price offered, reported, recorded, or otherwise encountered under identifiable conditions.

Its meaning shall include, where available:

- Economic Object
- Monetary amount
- Currency
- Unit of measurement
- Quantity
- Transaction stage
- Geographic applicability
- Observation date
- Commercial terms
- Evidence reference

A Price Observation shall preserve whether the price represents an offer, quotation, awarded contract, invoice, payment, published benchmark, or other economic event.

These categories shall not be silently conflated.

### 5.3 Economic Context

An Economic Context represents the conditions under which an economic observation or claim is meaningful.

It may include geography, time, availability, transaction volume, delivery conditions, environmental exposure, local capacity, commercial terms, and applicable technical requirements.

Context shall be treated as a constituent of economic meaning rather than an optional annotation.

### 5.4 Cost Component

A Cost Component represents an identifiable contribution to the cost of an Economic Object or development activity.

It may include materials, labor, equipment, logistics, mobilization, overhead, taxes, financing, risk allowances, and other relevant expenditures.

Cost Components shall support the decomposition of aggregate estimates wherever sufficient evidence exists.

Unknown decomposition shall remain explicitly unknown.

The Atlas shall not invent component allocations merely to create the appearance of analytical completeness.

### 5.5 Market Signal

A Market Signal represents evidence of a condition or movement that may influence economic prices.

Examples include:

- Fuel price movements
- Exchange rate changes
- Regional wage adjustments
- Material shortages
- Changes in supplier capacity
- Increased construction demand
- Shipping disruptions
- Changes in government policy
- Changes in market competition

A Market Signal shall not automatically constitute evidence of a corresponding change in project cost.

Its relevance and transmission into particular Cost Components shall be examined.

### 5.6 Contextual Factor

A Contextual Factor represents a condition that may influence the practical cost, feasibility, duration, or risk of an economic activity.

Examples include:

- Difficult terrain
- Island accessibility
- Weather-related interruptions
- Distance from material sources
- Limited equipment availability
- Geological uncertainty
- Restricted construction windows

A Contextual Factor is not inherently a monetary adjustment.

Its financial influence must be established through evidence, appropriate technical analysis, or a disclosed estimation process.

### 5.7 Economic Claim

An Economic Claim is an assertion concerning price, cost, economic conditions, comparability, or the interpretation of observed economic information.

Examples include:

- A particular supplier offers cement at a stated price.
- Aggregate transportation costs are higher in a particular locality.
- A historical benchmark is no longer representative of current market conditions.
- A proposed infrastructure cost falls within a defensible range.

Economic Claims shall be subject to inherited mechanisms of provenance, warrants, deliberation, and accountability.

### 5.8 Cost Estimate

A Cost Estimate is a derived representation of anticipated expenditure under stated assumptions and conditions.

It may be assembled from historical benchmarks, contemporary observations, component models, economic adjustments, contextual factors, and professional judgment.

A Cost Estimate shall remain distinguishable from an observed transaction or realized expenditure.

Its precision shall be proportionate to the quality and completeness of its inputs.

### 5.9 Reasonable Cost Envelope

A Reasonable Cost Envelope is a bounded representation of the costs that may be reasonably supported by available evidence for a sufficiently defined Economic Object and Economic Context.

It may include:

- A lower plausible cost
- A central estimate, where justified
- An upper plausible cost
- Conditions affecting the range
- Evidence quality
- Unresolved uncertainties
- Geographic and temporal applicability

An envelope shall not constitute a legally binding minimum, maximum, or procurement ceiling.

Nor shall an expenditure outside the envelope independently establish irregularity.

The envelope represents the current extent of warranted economic understanding.

### 5.10 Realization Record

A Realization Record represents the observed outcome of an economic activity or development project.

It shall seek to preserve the relationship between anticipated expenditure, realized expenditure, delivered scope, execution conditions, and observed performance.

The Realization Record shall provide a mechanism through which earlier estimates may be assessed against experience.

It shall contribute to the continuous refinement of economic understanding.

## 6. The Price as a Contextual Claim

The Atlas shall not treat a price as an isolated scalar value.

A meaningful economic price shall be understood through the relationship between:

- Object
- Specification
- Amount
- Unit
- Quantity
- Time
- Place
- Commercial conditions
- Evidence
- Context

Two numerically identical prices may represent materially different economic realities.

Two numerically different prices may represent equivalent economic realities after relevant conditions are accounted for.

Consequently, the Atlas shall distinguish between normalization and equivalence.

Normalization makes observations easier to examine.

Equivalence establishes whether those observations can legitimately be compared.

The former shall not automatically imply the latter.

---

## 7. Contextual Comparability

### 7.1 Technical Comparability

Economic observations shall be assessed according to the technical equivalence of the objects they represent.

For infrastructure works, technical comparability may require examination of pavement thickness, material strength, carriageway width, earthworks, drainage provisions, terrain classifications, design standards, and other material specifications.

A shared project description shall not independently establish comparability.

### 7.2 Commercial Comparability

Commercial terms shall be considered when comparing prices.

An ex-warehouse price shall not automatically be compared with a delivered price.

A wholesale price shall not automatically be compared with a retail price.

A tax-exclusive price shall not automatically be compared with a tax-inclusive price.

A quotation shall not automatically be treated as equivalent to a completed transaction.

### 7.3 Geographic Comparability

Geographic proximity alone shall not establish economic equivalence.

Two nearby municipalities may exhibit substantially different construction costs due to differences in road access, maritime dependencies, local suppliers, equipment availability, or terrain.

Geographic intelligence shall therefore account for the practical relationships through which goods, labor, and services move.

### 7.4 Temporal Comparability

Economic observations shall be assessed according to their temporal relevance.

Historical prices may be adjusted to facilitate contemporary comparison, but such adjustment shall remain a derived operation.

The Atlas shall preserve both the original observation and its adjusted representation.

### 7.5 Comparability as a Claim

A determination that two observations are comparable shall itself constitute an Economic Claim.

Where material to a published conclusion, that determination shall possess traceable justification.

The assumptions underlying comparability shall remain open to examination and challenge.

## 8. Context as First-Class Knowledge

Conventional cost systems frequently treat local conditions as exceptions to generalized price assumptions.

The Atlas shall adopt a different position.

Context shall be recognized as a normal and constituent part of economic reality.

Geography, logistics, weather, market access, and institutional conditions shall not be relegated to unstructured remarks when they materially influence cost.

However, the Atlas shall distinguish between:

1. The existence of a contextual condition.
2. The relevance of that condition to an activity.
3. The magnitude of its influence.
4. The financial implications of that influence.

Evidence that a municipality experiences frequent rainfall does not independently establish a particular construction cost premium.

Such a premium may require evidence of reduced workable days, mobilization interruptions, equipment idling, additional protective measures, or other attributable costs.

The Atlas shall seek to establish the relationships through which contextual conditions become economically consequential.

**The presence of difficulty shall not automatically warrant a price increase. The economic consequences of difficulty must be understood.**

---

## 9. Economic Dynamics and Market Forces

The Atlas shall recognize that economic knowledge cannot be maintained solely through periodic inflation adjustment.

Prices emerge from dynamic relationships involving supply, demand, availability, production capacity, transportation, competition, policy, and external economic conditions.

Historical baselines shall provide continuity.

Economic indices shall provide directional intelligence.

Contemporary observations shall provide market evidence.

Contextual analysis shall establish applicability.

Realization Records shall provide experience.

These sources shall complement one another without being treated as interchangeable.

Where sufficient component-level knowledge exists, economic adjustments should be applied to the relevant Cost Components.

Where such knowledge is unavailable, aggregate adjustments may be used provisionally, with their limitations explicitly disclosed.

The Atlas shall guard against double-counting where a Market Signal or Contextual Factor is already reflected in an observed price or another adjustment.

Market forces shall be represented through their demonstrated or reasonably inferred effects, rather than through arbitrary multipliers.

## 10. The Architecture of Estimation

The Atlas shall distinguish three broad pathways through which contemporary cost intelligence may be produced.

### 10.1 Historical Projection

Historical Projection shall derive contemporary estimates from preserved historical benchmarks and disclosed economic adjustments.

It shall provide a provisional mechanism where contemporary market evidence remains sparse.

Historical Projection shall not be represented as direct observation.

### 10.2 Market Reconstruction

Market Reconstruction shall assemble estimates from contemporary evidence concerning the costs of relevant materials, labor, equipment, logistics, and other components.

Its reliability shall depend upon the technical completeness, comparability, coverage, and quality of the contributing evidence.

Market Reconstruction shall not presume that the availability of individual prices establishes the completeness of an entire project estimate.

### 10.3 Experience-Based Calibration

Experience-Based Calibration shall use relevant realized costs and project outcomes to assess and improve estimates.

Historical project experience shall be examined in relation to differences in scope, quality, execution conditions, market conditions, and time.

Actual expenditure shall inform estimation without automatically becoming the definition of reasonable expenditure.

### 10.4 Convergent Estimation

Where multiple estimation pathways are available, the Atlas may compare their respective conclusions.

Convergence may strengthen confidence.

Divergence may reveal limitations, missing components, changing conditions, or unresolved assumptions.

The Atlas shall investigate material divergence rather than conceal it through indiscriminate averaging.

The purpose of multiple estimation pathways is not merely to produce another number.

It is to create opportunities for independent examination of economic understanding.

## 11. Domain Relationships

Through the applicable Ugnayan architecture, the Atlas shall recognize relationships among economic participants.

Initial participant profiles may include:

| Participant | Primary domain contribution |
|---|---|
| Supplier | Offered prices, product specifications, availability and commercial conditions |
| Manufacturer | Production characteristics, capacity and product information |
| Logistics provider | Transportation costs, routes and delivery constraints |
| Contractor | Execution costs, methods and operational experience |
| Engineer | Technical interpretation and comparability |
| Government institution | Official benchmarks, procurement records and public infrastructure knowledge |
| Community | Situated observations and local accessibility conditions |
| Researcher | Methodological analysis and independent examination |
| Domain steward | Evidence integrity, validation and responsible publication |
| Public user | Application, examination, challenge and contribution |

These profiles shall not be treated as permanent hierarchies of credibility.

An individual or institution may participate through multiple profiles, subject to appropriate disclosure and conflict-of-interest controls.

Authority shall arise from the applicable role, evidence, competence, and governing authorization.

The relationships established within the Atlas shall enable collaboration without transferring unrestricted authority to any participant.

## 12. Participation Without Architectural Awareness

Participants shall not be required to understand Wisdom Infrastructure, ZenOS, Ugnayan, or the internal architecture of the Living Price Atlas before receiving value from it.

A supplier should be able to contribute a quotation through an accessible interface.

An engineer should be able to submit a bill of quantities without reconstructing it into a proprietary data format.

A contractor should be able to document an unexpected site condition without learning the domain ontology.

A community member should be able to describe a practical accessibility problem in ordinary language.

The Atlas shall assume responsibility for interpreting and organizing these contributions while preserving their evidential origins and uncertainties.

Accessible interaction shall not imply unaccountable ingestion.

The necessary checks, permissions, validation, and safeguards shall remain operative beneath the participant-facing experience.

**The complexity of trustworthy intelligence shall be borne by the infrastructure, not unnecessarily transferred to its participants.**

---

## 13. Distributed Economic Sensing

The Atlas shall develop as a distributed economic sensing infrastructure.

Its participants shall collectively expand the visibility of economic conditions that no single institution can continuously observe.

Suppliers encounter changes in availability and pricing.

Contractors encounter changes in execution conditions.

Engineers encounter discrepancies between generalized assumptions and technical realities.

Communities encounter local constraints that may remain invisible within formal datasets.

Public institutions encounter procurement outcomes across numerous development activities.

Each participant possesses a partial view of economic reality.

The Atlas shall provide a means through which these partial views can become mutually informative.

However, participation density shall not be confused with market representativeness.

The Atlas shall account for geographic coverage gaps, commercial concentration, selective disclosure, correlated evidence, and systematic exclusion of less visible market participants.

The number of observations shall not independently establish the completeness of economic knowledge.

---

## 14. Reciprocal Value

The continuity of the Atlas shall depend upon its ability to provide meaningful value to those who participate in it.

Participation shall not be designed solely as the extraction of information from economic actors.

Where appropriate, contributors may receive useful intelligence in return, including:

- Historical cost comparisons
- Aggregated market movements
- Contextual construction estimates
- Procurement planning intelligence
- Identification of regional knowledge gaps
- Improved understanding of logistics constraints
- Feedback on the completeness and applicability of contributed evidence

Reciprocal value shall not require the disclosure of commercially sensitive information.

Nor shall contribution volume entitle participants to favorable treatment, privileged conclusions, or access to information that could facilitate market manipulation.

The Atlas shall seek sustainable participation through utility and trust rather than coercion or dependence.

## 15. Artificial Intelligence as Domain Interpreter

Artificial intelligence within the Atlas shall inherit the boundaries established by LPA001 and the applicable foundational architecture.

Its domain-specific responsibilities may include:

- Recognizing Economic Objects in heterogeneous evidence.
- Extracting Price Observations.
- Identifying commercial and technical conditions.
- Discovering potential comparability relationships.
- Interpreting Market Signals.
- Identifying Contextual Factors.
- Assisting in the decomposition of infrastructure costs.
- Generating provisional Cost Estimates.
- Explaining discrepancies between estimation pathways.
- Identifying missing or contradictory evidence.
- Supporting deliberation and knowledge refinement.

AI-generated domain interpretations shall not silently replace source evidence.

Where deterministic calculations are sufficient, their results shall remain reproducible without dependence upon the generative behavior of an AI model.

Where professional judgment is necessary, the system shall identify the relevant need for human expertise.

AI shall not be permitted to manufacture economic certainty where evidence remains incomplete.

The Atlas shall use intelligence to expand the visibility of reality, not to obscure the limitations of its representation.

## 16. The Evidence-to-Intelligence Lifecycle

The domain architecture shall support a continuous lifecycle consisting of the following stages.

### 16.1 Encounter

An economic participant, institution, or system encounters information concerning prices, costs, conditions, or outcomes.

### 16.2 Contribution

The information is made available to the Atlas through an appropriate participation mechanism.

### 16.3 Preservation

The original evidence is preserved with its relevant provenance, permissions, and historical context.

### 16.4 Interpretation

The evidence is interpreted into applicable domain semantics.

### 16.5 Comparison

The interpreted information is examined against relevant observations, specifications, and contextual conditions.

### 16.6 Justification

Material Economic Claims are examined through the applicable warrant and validation mechanisms.

### 16.7 Synthesis

Warranted knowledge is assembled into Cost Estimates, Reasonable Cost Envelopes, historical comparisons, and other forms of economic intelligence.

### 16.8 Publication

Intelligence is made accessible according to appropriate disclosure, privacy, accountability, and governance requirements.

### 16.9 Application

Human participants and institutions apply the intelligence within their legitimate decision-making responsibilities.

### 16.10 Realization

Observed outcomes are documented and associated with the applicable estimates, claims, and contextual assumptions.

### 16.11 Reconsideration

New evidence and realized experience may initiate correction, deliberation, recalibration, or the development of improved methodologies.

The lifecycle shall remain continuous.

Publication shall not constitute the termination of inquiry.

## 17. The Domain Intelligence Contract

Every material intelligence output of the Atlas shall seek to communicate:

1. What economic question is being addressed.
2. What Economic Objects are involved.
3. What geographic and temporal context applies.
4. What evidence supports the conclusion.
5. What estimation or interpretive method was employed.
6. What assumptions were introduced.
7. What contextual factors materially influence the conclusion.
8. What uncertainties and contradictory evidence remain.
9. What limitations govern the appropriate use of the output.
10. How its underlying knowledge may be examined or challenged.

The presentation of this information may be adapted to the needs and capabilities of the intended audience.

A concise public interface need not expose every technical detail simultaneously.

However, simplification of presentation shall not become concealment of epistemic limitations.

The underlying intelligence shall remain inspectable to the extent permitted by legitimate access restrictions.

## 18. Historical Instantiation: DPWH Price Atlas 2022

The initial Philippine implementation shall begin with the DPWH Price Atlas 2022.

This source shall serve as a historical institutional reference within the Atlas.

Its ingestion shall establish the first practical application of the domain architecture.

### 18.1 Preservation

Available source artifacts shall be preserved independently of extracted and normalized representations.

The currently available CSV shall be identified as an extracted representation rather than automatically presumed to be the definitive original institutional publication.

### 18.2 Normalization

Infrastructure descriptions, specifications, regions, units, and historical benchmark values shall be interpreted into applicable domain semantics.

Ambiguities, duplicate headers, inconsistencies, and extraction errors shall be explicitly recorded.

### 18.3 Institutional Reference Classification

The historical values shall be classified according to their actual evidential nature.

They shall not be represented as individual supplier transactions or independently verified contemporary market prices.

### 18.4 Provisional Contemporary Estimates

The Atlas may generate a provisional 2026 reference layer through relevant economic adjustments.

The selected indices, reference periods, calculations, assumptions, and limitations shall remain inspectable.

Where contemporary regional observations are absent, the resulting knowledge shall explicitly retain its provisional character.

### 18.5 Progressive Enrichment

Contemporary supplier evidence, technical assessments, contextual observations, and realized project costs shall progressively enrich the historical foundation.

The original benchmark shall remain preserved.

The transition from historical reference to living intelligence shall occur through the accumulation of evidence rather than the replacement of historical values.

## 19. Progressive Domain Maturity

The Atlas shall permit meaningful operation before comprehensive economic knowledge becomes available.

Its initial maturity may progress through the following states.

| Maturity state | Distinguishing capability |
|---|---|
| Historical | Preserved and normalized historical references |
| Projected | Transparent economic adjustment of historical knowledge |
| Observational | Incorporation of contemporary market observations |
| Contextual | Representation of material local and operational conditions |
| Corroborative | Independent examination and comparison of economic claims |
| Calibrated | Refinement through realized economic and project outcomes |
| Continuously learning | Sustained participation, revision and accountable knowledge accumulation |

These states shall not constitute a universal linear progression.

Different Economic Objects, localities, and infrastructure categories may exhibit different levels of maturity.

A well-observed urban construction market shall not confer equivalent confidence upon a sparsely observed island municipality.

The maturity of the platform shall remain distinguishable from the maturity of any particular body of economic knowledge within it.

## 20. Geographic Instantiation

The initial operational implementation may concentrate upon Eastern Visayas.

The region presents diverse economic and contextual conditions, including island geography, varying market accessibility, maritime transportation dependencies, environmental exposure, and differences in local construction capacity.

These conditions provide a meaningful environment in which the domain architecture may be exercised and refined.

An initial implementation may prioritize:

- Road construction and rehabilitation
- Farm-to-market roads
- Drainage infrastructure
- Flood mitigation
- Slope protection
- Associated construction materials and services

The regional implementation shall not be treated as a restriction upon the national applicability of the architecture.

Its purpose shall be to establish domain understanding through practical experience before expanding the scale of deployment.

Regional experience shall inform subsequent implementations without being presumed universally applicable.

## 21. Foundational Feedback

Inheritance shall not be exclusively unidirectional.

The Living Price Atlas may encounter requirements, ambiguities, and operational conditions that reveal limitations or opportunities within the underlying foundation.

Examples may include:

- Economic claims with rapidly changing applicability.
- Commercial evidence requiring differentiated disclosure.
- Correlated observations originating from related market actors.
- Contextual warrants spanning multiple geographic conditions.
- Deliberation involving technical and economic expertise.
- Historical reconstruction under incomplete market visibility.

Where such requirements have significance beyond the economic domain, they may be proposed for consideration within the foundational architecture.

The Atlas shall not unilaterally redefine inherited mechanisms.

Foundational improvements shall proceed through the applicable deliberation and governance processes.

Domain experience shall enrich the foundation while preserving the coherence of its shared architecture.

**Inheritance shall create a relationship of mutual learning between the foundation and its realizations.**

---

## 22. Architectural Portability

The Living Price Atlas shall remain faithful to LPA001 independently of the continued availability of any particular technical implementation.

The adoption of ZenOS and Ugnayan shall not diminish the requirement for institutional and technological independence.

Economic knowledge shall remain capable of being preserved, interpreted, transferred, and historically reconstructed through suitable mechanisms.

Where an inherited capability is unavailable, incomplete, or unsuitable for a particular domain requirement, the deficiency shall be explicitly identified.

It may be addressed through:

- An applicable foundational extension.
- A bounded domain specialization.
- A transparent interim implementation.
- A limitation upon the affected operational capability.

The absence of a capability shall not justify the silent abandonment of a foundational invariant.

Architectural inheritance shall be a source of continuity and coherence, not an instrument of dependency or capture.

---

## 23. Non-Responsibilities

The domain architecture shall not independently establish:

- The general-purpose epistemic framework of Wisdom Infrastructure.
- The foundational warrant semantics of ZenOS.
- The general deliberation architecture.
- The foundational identity and authority mechanisms.
- The general relational model of Ugnayan.
- Legally binding economic or procurement determinations.
- Professional engineering certification.
- Mandatory commercial pricing.
- Findings of wrongdoing unsupported by appropriate evidence and due process.

These responsibilities either belong to inherited foundational mechanisms or remain within the legitimate authority of human professionals and institutions.

The Atlas shall operate within these boundaries.

Its capacity to produce intelligence shall not be confused with the authority to impose decisions.

---

## 24. Architectural Invariants

All implementations of the Living Price Atlas shall preserve the foundational invariants established in LPA001.

In addition, the following domain architectural invariants shall apply.

**I. Foundational inheritance shall precede unnecessary duplication.**

**II. Domain specialization shall not silently redefine foundational semantics.**

**III. Economic observations shall preserve their contextual and temporal meaning.**

**IV. Technical normalization shall not automatically establish comparability.**

**V. A contextual condition shall not automatically warrant a monetary adjustment.**

**VI. Observed transactions, institutional references, derived estimates, and realized costs shall remain distinguishable.**

**VII. Economic intelligence shall remain proportionate to the quality and coverage of its supporting evidence.**

**VIII. Multiple estimation pathways shall remain independently examinable.**

**IX. Participation shall not require architectural sophistication.**

**X. Reciprocal value shall not compromise epistemic independence.**

**XI. Domain experience shall contribute to foundational improvement through accountable processes.**

**XII. Architectural inheritance shall not compromise institutional or technological independence.**

Where an implementation conflicts with these invariants, the implementation shall be reconsidered.

## Closing Declaration

The Living Price Atlas does not begin with the presumption that economic knowledge is absent.

It begins with the recognition that economic knowledge already exists, distributed among people, institutions, markets, places, and experiences.

What remains insufficient is the infrastructure through which that knowledge can be preserved, connected, examined, and responsibly applied.

Wisdom Infrastructure establishes the purpose.

ZenOS establishes the intended operating foundation.

Ugnayan establishes the intended relational architecture.

The Living Price Atlas establishes the economic domain through which these capabilities become meaningful in the practical work of development.

Together, they provide the conditions through which isolated economic observations may become shared economic understanding.

The responsibility of the Atlas is not to compel every participant to see the same reality.

It is to enable participants to bring their respective encounters with reality into a shared environment where understanding can deepen without sacrificing evidence, context, accountability, or legitimate disagreement.

The Atlas shall therefore be measured not merely by the volume of prices it contains, but by the quality of economic understanding it enables.

**We inherit the infrastructure of trustworthy intelligence so that each new domain may advance human understanding rather than rediscover its foundations.**
